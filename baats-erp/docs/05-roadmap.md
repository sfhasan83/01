# 05. Development roadmap, testing and deployment

## 1. Honest scope statement

The full vision (30 modules, 12 engines, 10 jurisdictions, web and mobile, SaaS operations) is comparable to products that took vendors many years and hundreds of engineers. The plan below delivers it in usable increments: every phase ends with working, tested, integrated software that a real organization can run, rather than many partly built modules.

Indicative effort with a core team of 25 to 40 engineers plus domain specialists: Phase 1 about 4 to 6 months, Phases 2 to 3 about 12 to 18 months, Phases 4 to 7 a further 18 to 24 months, overlapping where dependencies allow.

## 3. Phases

### Phase 1: Enterprise core (this pull request starts it)

**Built and tested now:** multi-tenant platform with database-enforced isolation and automated onboarding; configurable org hierarchy (levels, units, cost and profit centers, designations, employees, reporting lines, org chart); identity (passwords, TOTP MFA, lockout, sessions, revocation); RBAC scoped to org subtrees with ABAC conditions, SoD and delegation; tamper-evident audit trail; master data (UoM, items, customers and suppliers with governed activation and duplicate detection); no-code workflow engine (sequential, parallel, conditional, quorum, limits, maker-checker, delegation, escalation, versioning, history); REST API; CI.

**Phase 1b, to finish the core:** SSO (OIDC, SAML) and Active Directory, SCIM provisioning; notification engine (email, SMS, WhatsApp, push) with templates; document engine (object storage, versions, retention); transactional outbox and event bus; custom-field metadata registry; record- and field-level permissions; i18n framework; admin web console (org chart, roles, workflow designer); OpenAPI docs; rate limiting; tenant export and restore.

**Acceptance:** two tenants cannot read or write each other's data under any API or SQL path; every change is in a verifiable audit chain; an approval matrix can be changed by an administrator without a release; 50k-employee synthetic tenant meets latency targets for org and permission queries.

### Phase 2: Core business

**Built and tested:** finance (accounting engine, UAE VAT and corporate tax, receivables, payables, cash, statements, period close) on BAATS Accounting's UAE rules; inventory; procurement (approved purchase orders, receipts, three-way match); sales and CRM (leads to cash); HR and payroll (contracts, leave, payroll, WPS, gratuity). Procure-to-pay, order-to-cash, hire-to-pay and record-to-report run end to end with automatic postings, and the trial balance and stock valuation are tested to agree. E-invoicing (PINT AE), bank reconciliation and fixed assets are also built. Still open in this phase: transmission through an accredited e-invoicing provider, PDC cheques and VAT on advances, IFRS 16 leases, prepayments and accruals; see [06-finance-and-baats-accounting.md](06-finance-and-baats-accounting.md). KSA and other country packs are Phase 6.

Accounting engine and finance (GL, AP, AR, cash and bank, fixed assets lite, period close, consolidation, IFRS reports); taxation (VAT, GST, corporate tax, withholding; UAE and KSA first, e-invoicing with FTA and ZATCA); HR (positions, contracts, leave, attendance with biometric import); payroll (UAE WPS and KSA GOSI first); procurement; inventory; sales and CRM. Integrate BAATS Accounting rules once its source or specification is supplied.

**Acceptance:** procure-to-pay, order-to-cash, hire-to-retire and record-to-report run end to end with automatic postings and no re-keying; trial balance always balances; tax returns reconcile to the ledger.

### Phase 3: Operations

**Built and tested:** projects (ledger dimension, timesheets costed from contracts, time and material and milestone billing, budget against actuals); manufacturing (work centers, BOMs with scrap and routing, cost roll-up, work orders through WIP at actual cost with backflush and overhead absorption, reorder minimums, multi-level MRP); maintenance (preventive plans by date or distance, work orders, spare parts to repairs and maintenance); fleet (vehicles linked to fixed assets, fuel with reclaimable VAT, trips, fuel economy, expiry alerts). Still open in this phase: subcontracted manufacturing, by-products and standard costing with variances; project revenue recognition over time (IFRS 15 percentage of completion) and project expenses claimed by employees; lot and serial tracking, landed costs and bin locations; logistics (shipments, carriers, routes) and GPS telematics.

Manufacturing (BOM, routing, MRP, work orders, costing), projects, supply chain and logistics, assets and maintenance, fleet.

### Phase 4: Corporate functions

**Built and tested:** document management (versioned files with SHA-256 fingerprints, attachable to any record, confidential access, retention, archive without deletion); legal and contracts (approval workflow requiring the signed copy, obligations, notice dates, automatic renewal or expiry, termination); risk register (inherent and residual scores, appetite, 5 x 5 heat map), controls and control tests by someone other than the owner; compliance calendar with recurring obligations; internal audit engagements with findings, management responses and opinions; one corrective action list shared by audit, risk, quality and safety with maker-checker verification; IT and facilities service desk with SLA targets, breaches, requester confirmation and hand-off to maintenance; quality inspections on receipt, production or stock, non-conformance reports whose scrap or return disposition posts the write-off and drafts the supplier credit note; health and safety incidents open to everyone, investigations, LTIFR and TRIR; facilities spaces, bookings without double booking, and utilisation. Still open in this phase: learning management, customer service portal, collaboration, board and corporate secretariat, e-signature and OCR of uploaded documents.

Legal and contracts, GRC, audit management, administration and facilities, IT service management, quality, HSE, document management UI, learning, customer service, collaboration, board and corporate secretariat.

### Phase 5: Intelligence

**Built and tested (5a, analytics):** a KPI engine of 36 measures computed live from the ledger and every module, each guarded by its source module's permission; the executive dashboard (month and year to date against prior periods, 12-month trends, top customers, overdue balances); a report builder over a governed dataset catalogue (grouping, totals, filters, sort, CSV export, saved and shared reports) that only returns rows from the units a user's roles cover; a strategy scorecard of objectives and KPI targets with pro-rated red, amber and green status; ESG scope 1 and 2 emissions from fuel logs and meter readings with overridable factors, resource use and intensity; anomaly checks over the whole ledger (duplicate bills, weekend and round-amount manual journals, late postings, credit-note ratios, unmatched payments, Benford's law) with independent review. **Built and tested (5b, assistant):** the BAATS AI assistant behind a provider layer (Claude by default; OpenAI-compatible services and local models as alternatives), answering from 16 read tools that run as the user, and proposing changes through 3 tools that run only after the user confirms; encrypted provider keys, a monthly allowance, private-address protection for hosted deployments, and an audited record of every tool call. Next in this phase: document reading (extracting invoices and contracts), forecasting, and more assistant actions as each module's workflows allow. Still open: R&D management, board packs, a data warehouse for very large tenants.

Data warehouse and BI, dashboard and report builders, executive and board dashboards, strategy and performance, R&D, ESG, the BAATS AI assistant (permission-aware queries, document processing, forecasting, anomaly and fraud detection, automated reconciliation, predictive maintenance, decision support).

### Phase 6: Reach

**Built and tested (6a):** the interactive dashboard (any period, from presets on the financial year or custom dates, against the previous period or the same period last year; each KPI drills down to its month-by-month line with the comparison, breakdowns by customer, supplier, account, cost center, vehicle, department or age, and the records behind it; each user keeps their own tiles, order, period and comparison per company; the assistant can break a KPI down too). The country-pack layer: chart, tax codes, VAT return form, tax number and IBAN formats, e-invoice standard, social insurance and the end-of-service rule come from the company's country, with the UAE moved onto it unchanged. The Saudi Arabia pack: books in SAR with 15% VAT and ZATCA exemption reasons, the ZATCA VAT return (boxes 1 to 16; GCC sales, customs VAT, corrections and carried-forward credit are entered on the portal), ZATCA e-invoices (UBL 2.1 in ZATCA's layout, standard and simplified, invoice counter, hash chain, QR code with fields 1 to 6, and a chain check), GOSI by nationality from rates each company can correct, and the Saudi end-of-service award reduced on resignation. Still open for Saudi Arabia: ZATCA onboarding, cryptographic stamping and clearance or reporting through ZATCA's API (QR fields 7 to 9), zakat and income tax, the Mudad payroll file. 

**Built and tested (6b):** money in three decimals: every amount column keeps three decimals and each amount is rounded to its own currency (the company's base currency for the ledger, the document's currency for invoice lines and payments), so dinars and rials keep their fils and baisa while two-decimal currencies are unchanged. The rest of the GCC: Oman (OMR; 5% VAT with the Oman VAT return by its sections; 15% income tax through the existing provision; the Social Protection Fund for Omanis and work injury for everyone; one month of basic a year for expatriates), Bahrain (BHD; 10% VAT and the NBR return, boxes 1 to 17; no general corporate tax; SIO pension, unemployment and injury, and the expatriate leaving indemnity at 4.2% rising to 8.4% after three years, with the employer owing the labour-law award only for service before March 2024), Qatar (QAR; no VAT; GRSIA for Qataris; three weeks of basic a year after the first year) and Kuwait (KWD; no VAT; PIFSS for Kuwaitis; fifteen days then a month a year, at most 18 months, reduced on resignation). Nationals covered by a state pension get no end-of-service award in these four. Social insurance rates can step up with years of service. Still open: the WPS salary files of Oman, Bahrain, Qatar and Kuwait; Oman's e-invoicing (Fawtara) once it is in force; Qatar and Kuwait income tax, zakat and NLST; GCC nationals working in another GCC state (the GCC extension of social insurance); the Oman 3% small-business tax rate. The mobile app and integrations followed in 6c.

**Built and tested (6c):** BAATS Mobile, a phone web app served by the BAATS server at `/m/` and added to the home screen, for approvals (with the claim and its receipt photos), leave balance and requests, payslips and expense claims with the camera; employee self-service limited to the user's own record. Expense claims with receipts, line-manager approval, posting with input VAT and payment from the bank. API keys that act as a named user, and webhooks for invoices, payments, payroll, expense claims, leave and tickets, written through an outbox in the same transaction, signed with HMAC-SHA256 and retried with backoff. The Windows trial can let phones on the same Wi-Fi reach it (off by default) and has a built-in webhook test receiver. Still open: store apps for Android and iOS (the web app covers the same work but offline use and notifications need https), push notifications, named connectors (banks, payment gateways, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, e-commerce, e-signature), OAuth for third-party apps. Next in this phase: further country packs and the connectors.

**Built and tested (6d, connectors):** bank statements as MT940 and CAMT.053 files (the account recognised by IBAN, balances carried) into the existing reconciliation; supplier payment files in ISO 20022 pain.001.001.03 with IBAN checks and one live file per payment; sign-in with Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace or any OpenID Connect provider (PKCE, signed ID tokens, allowed domains, existing users only, MFA carried over); email through SMTP or Microsoft 365 with an outbox and retries, sales invoices sent as PDF with the e-invoice XML, and approval notices; Stripe card payment links on sales invoices that record and allocate the receipt when paid (by polling, or Stripe's signed notice where the server is reachable); Excel (.xlsx) files in the data import. Tested against stand-in servers for SMTP, OpenID, Microsoft Graph and Stripe, not yet against the real services or real bank files. Still open: host-to-host bank connections (SWIFT, bank APIs) and bank-specific formats beyond pain.001, payment gateways other than Stripe (Network International, Checkout.com, PayTabs, HyperPay), Google Workspace mail, calendar and drive, e-commerce and e-signature connectors, Arabic text in the invoice PDF (it needs an embedded Arabic font), OAuth for third-party apps.

**Built and tested (6e, the UK and the EU):** packs for the United Kingdom, Ireland, Germany, France, the Netherlands, Spain and Italy with their charts, VAT codes, tax number and IBAN formats; VAT returns for all seven from the ledger (HMRC nine boxes with the Making Tax Digital body, VAT3, UStVA, CA3, OB, 303, LIPE) and the EC sales list; corporate tax with UK marginal relief and the French and Dutch bands; XRechnung and Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoices; payroll with UK PAYE and National Insurance, Irish PAYE, USC and PRSI, contributions between thresholds and caps, income tax at each employee's rate elsewhere, SEPA salary files and the French, Dutch, Spanish and Italian end-of-service rules. Still open: sending returns and payroll filings to the authorities (MTD submission, RTI, Revenue PAYE, ELSTER, DSN, loonaangifte, SILTRA, UniEmens), the German, Dutch, Spanish and Italian wage tax calculations, FatturaPA, Factur-X and VeriFactu, statutory redundancy pay in the UK and Ireland, Belgium and the other EU states.
