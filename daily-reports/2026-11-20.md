### 🆕 Trading card accessories (Amazon.com)
- **Product idea:** Start with **one hero product**: a **9-pocket zip binder (360 cards)** with side-loading pockets and a padded cover, or a **matte card sleeves + toploaders bundle**. Then build a range (deck boxes, magnetic one-touch holders, graded-card cases).
- **Unit economics (9-pocket zip binder at $24.99):**

  | Item | Amount |
  |---|---|
  | Referral fee (15%) | −$3.75 |
  | FBA fee (large standard, ~1.2 lb, estimate) | −$5.20 |
  | Landed cost (estimate) | −$4.50 |
  | Ads (~15%) | −$3.75 |
  | **Net profit / unit** | **≈ $7.80 (≈ 31%)** |

- **Differentiation angle:** The main complaints about cheap binders are cards slipping out, rings bending cards, and pockets that don't fit thicker sleeved cards. Use **side-loading, ring-free pockets** sized for sleeved cards, a strong zip, and acid-free PVC-free material (collectors care about this). Show it full of cards in the main image.
- **Validate before ordering:** check the top 10 for "trading card binder 9 pocket" and "card sleeves" (review counts, prices); check that you never use Pokémon, Magic or Yu-Gi-Oh names or artwork (trademark risk). "Fits standard trading cards" is the safe wording.
- **Risks:** Big brands (Ultra Pro, Dragon Shield, Vault X) lead; win on design and value, not price. Avoid pictures of branded cards in your images.
